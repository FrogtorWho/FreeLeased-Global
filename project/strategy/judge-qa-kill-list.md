## 1. "Why is this not a chatbot?"

**Short answer.** The Fairness Check is a *citation* problem, not a
*generation* problem. The model doesn't need to invent language; it
needs to point at the correct section of the correct Act. A
deterministic regex + lookup table does that faster, cheaper, and
with provable provenance.

**The detail.** Tier-1 is fully deterministic — 25+ regex patterns
in [`src/lib/fairness.ts`](../../src/lib/fairness.ts:61), each with
a citation, an evidence class, and a confidence cap. Tier-2 is the
RAG-agentic fallback for the edge cases the patterns don't catch.
Tier-3 is the consensus gate that compares Tier-1 vs Tier-2. Tier-4
is the human sign-off queue. Total compute: **$0.00 per lease**.

**Rehearsal beat (15s).** "Citation problem, not generation problem.
Deterministic rules do the bulk. LLM is the fallback, capped at
heuristic. Sign-off is human. $0 compute."

**The detail.** This is the honest disclosure. The platform is at

base. We have 7 MoU partnerships on record with Caribbean
government agencies; one of them converts into a real pilot by
submission, that lifts the answer from "no" to "yes, one."

**Rehearsal beat (15s).** "TRL 5, not TRL 9. Synthetic cohort of
50 residents. One real pilot by submission closes this. We will
not claim otherwise."

## 3. "What's your moat?"

**Short answer.** Three moats, named explicitly:

1. **The growing verified spine** — a data network effect. Every
   statute + source + jurisdiction we add makes the next mapping
   cheaper. The cost curve trends to near-zero.
2. **The registry / MoU relationships** — defensive. We have 7
   Caribbean government MoUs on record; new entrants would have to
   rebuild those relationships.
3. **The honesty-engine IP** — the evidence-class caps + consensus
   gate. No incumbent ships honesty as a feature. This is the
   category-defining claim.

**The detail.** Moat 1 compounds with every jurisdiction. Moat 2
is relationship-bound and hard to clone. Moat 3 is the intellectual
property — the type-level constructs that enforce evidence-class
discipline are not easily replicated.

**Rehearsal beat (20s).** "Three moats. Data network effect.
Relationship moat. Honesty-engine IP. The third is the one no
incumbent has."

## 4. "Why should we believe the $0-compute claim?"

**Short answer.** Because the deterministic engine doesn't call any
external API. The Fairness Check is regex + lookup. The consensus
gate is in-process. The sign-off queue is a local Prisma database.
We have 231 passing tests that exercise every claim, and the test
suite runs in <5s on a laptop.

**The detail.** The Tier-2 RAG-agentic fallback *would* use inference,
but it has not been triggered in any test or pilot fixture — the
deterministic rules cover all the labelled cases. When Tier-2
*does* fire, it's grounded in retrieval and capped at heuristic
evidence class.

**Rehearsal beat (15s).** "231 tests, deterministic, $0. Tier-2
is grounded, capped, and unused in the pilot. Receipt: the
`scripts/generate-sample-dossier.ts` runs at $0.00 per execution."

## 7. "How do you handle conflicting statutes across jurisdictions?"

**Short answer.** The jurisdiction tag is part of the rule. A
Barbados rule does not fire on a UK lease. A UK rule does not fire
on a BB lease. The rules are jurisdiction-scoped, and the
[`src/lib/fairness.ts`](../../src/lib/fairness.ts) `ruleApplies`
function enforces the scope.

**The detail.** When a clause genuinely conflicts across
jurisdictions (e.g., a clause that's lawful in one and unlawful in
another), the system runs the rule per the *user-stated
jurisdiction*. The honest disclosure includes the jurisdiction
choice. The user can override the default.

**Rehearsal beat (15s).** "Jurisdiction-scoped rules. The
ruleApplies function enforces scope. Cross-jurisdiction conflict
runs per the user-stated jurisdiction."

## 8. "What happens when the consensus gate diverges?"

**Short answer.** The divergent claim is downgraded to `contested`
evidence class and routed to the sign-off queue. It is never
surfaced as a fact. A human reviewer (caseworker, paralegal,
advice-line volunteer) approves, rejects, or annotates. The
decision is written to an immutable audit log. The resident has a
visible appeal path.

**The detail.** This is the entire reason the consensus gate
exists. The honest model is "the codified and the agentic can
disagree, and disagreement is the most informative outcome we can
produce." The system uses disagreement to improve — divergent
claims are fed back into the Tier-1 rule promotion queue as
labelled examples.

**Rehearsal beat (20s).** "Divergent → contested → human review.
Never surfaced as fact. Audit row written. Resident has an
appeal path."

## 9. "What is your pricing model?"

**Short answer.** Three tiers, all published:

- **Residents / advocates:** free, freemium → pro at £5/month for
  unlimited audits and dossier export.
- **Institutions** (govt housing agencies, DFIs): £500-2,000/month
  per institution, depending on seat count and jurisdiction count.
- **Insurers / lenders** (climate + title risk): usage-based
  pricing, 5-15p per parcel audited, volume discounts.

**The detail.** Free tier is genuinely free — the cost of running
it is $0 (deterministic, no inference). The pro tier covers
operational costs + a small margin. The institution tier is the
primary revenue driver. The insurer tier is the highest-margin
product. Unit economics: 16:1 LTV:CAC, 92% gross margin, $0
marginal cost.

**Rehearsal beat (20s).** "Three tiers. Residents free, institutions
£500-2K/month, insurers usage-based. Unit economics: 16:1 LTV:CAC,
92% gross margin, $0 marginal cost."
