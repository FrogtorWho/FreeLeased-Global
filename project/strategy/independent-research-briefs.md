# Independent Research Briefs — blind verification pack

**Date issued:** 2026-08-05
**For:** independent researchers with no prior knowledge of this project.
**Purpose:** obtain impartial, primary-source answers to a set of factual
questions. These briefs are deliberately written **without** the expected
answer, so findings are not steered.

---

## Standing instructions (apply to every brief)

1. **Do not assume any answer is expected.** There is no "right" result. A
   well-evidenced "the claim is false", "cannot be confirmed", or "the source
   does not exist" is exactly as valuable as a confirmation.
2. **Prefer primary sources.** Official legislation portals, government
   gazettes, court judgments, official dataset documentation, and official
   company/organisation pages rank above blogs, marketing copy, law-firm
   summaries, or news write-ups. Where you rely on a secondary source, say so.
3. **Quote and cite.** For each answer give the exact title/section, the URL or
   document reference, and the date you accessed it. Where possible paste the
   operative sentence verbatim.
4. **Separate fact from inference.** Mark each finding as:
   `confirmed (primary)` · `confirmed (secondary only)` · `partly confirmed` ·
   `contradicted` · `not found / unverifiable`.
5. **Note recency and currency.** State the version/revision/commencement date
   of any law, and whether it is currently in force as at the date of research.
6. **Do not fill gaps with assumptions.** If a detail cannot be located, say
   "not located" rather than reasoning toward a plausible value.
7. **Report ambiguity.** If a term has multiple meanings or a source is
   internally inconsistent, describe the ambiguity rather than resolving it
   silently.

Return format per brief: **Question → Finding (tagged) → Source(s) → Notes.**

## Brief 1 — UK: Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024, commencement & RTM

**Objective:** establish the current commencement status and substantive effect
of the early-commenced provisions.

Questions:
1. What is the full title and year of the Act, and where is its authoritative
   text published?
2. Which commencement regulations (statutory instruments), if any, have brought
   any of its provisions into force? Give SI number(s), title(s), and the
   date(s) provisions came into force.
3. For any provision concerning the **Right to Manage (RTM)** and the
   proportion of a building that may be in **non-residential / commercial use**
   while remaining RTM-eligible: what does the provision say, what section
   number is it, what was the position **before** the change, and what is the
   position **after**?
4. Are there provisions concerning who bears the **costs** of an RTM claim? If
   so, cite them.

Do **not** assume any provision is in force; verify each independently.

## Brief 2 — Barbados: condominium unit entitlement

**Objective:** identify how ownership share and voting/expense allocation are
defined for condominium units.

Questions:
1. What is the primary statute governing condominiums in Barbados (exact title,
   chapter/cap number)?
2. Is there a section defining a unit's **"entitlement"** or ownership share?
   Give the section number and quote it.
3. How is that entitlement expressed (e.g. a fraction, a percentage, a fixed
   number)? What is it used to determine (common expenses, voting weight, other)?

## Brief 3 — Cayman Islands: strata titles law

**Objective:** identify the governing statute and its decision thresholds.

Questions:
1. What is the exact current title of the strata titles statute, including any
   **revision year**, and what original law/number does it derive from?
2. What voting thresholds does it set for strata corporation decisions? In
   particular, distinguish any category requiring a **unanimous** resolution
   from categories requiring a **super-majority** or **ordinary** resolution,
   and quote the relevant provisions with section numbers.
3. If any specific percentage threshold (e.g. for structural changes) is
   claimed, verify it against the text and report whether the figure appears.

## Brief 9 — Aggregate "value-at-risk" methodology (methodological, not claim-checking)

**Objective:** establish what a defensible methodology would require — without
reference to any specific figure.

Questions:
1. What established methodologies exist for estimating aggregate financial
   "value-at-risk" or "value-at-stake" across a portfolio of residential
   buildings or leasehold interests?
2. What input data and assumptions would such an estimate require to be
   defensible (e.g. number of units, per-unit values, hazard/liability model,
   time horizon, discounting)?
3. As a sanity-check reference only: what is the approximate total value of the
   UK residential housing stock, per official statistics (ONS or equivalent),
   with citation? (This is to calibrate order-of-magnitude plausibility, not to
   validate any particular number.)
