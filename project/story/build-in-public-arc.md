# FreeLeased — Build-in-Public Story Arc

A narrative the organisers can reuse for marketing. It follows a real journey from problem to pilot, told in five beats.

## Beat 1 — The itch
A solo builder in the Caribbean real-estate track starts from a lived frustration: you cannot get a straight answer about a piece of land or a lease anywhere in the region. Records are on paper, registries do not talk, and a tenant has no way to know if a clause is even legal. The question that starts everything: what would it take to let the Caribbean see its own property market clearly?

## Beat 2 — The wall
Early on, the build hits the same wall every serious team hits here: there is no land-data API to call. The organisers provide inference (the Impala gateway, one open model) and compute, but no parcel or registry data. Most teams would narrow their ambition. Instead the constraint becomes the design: if the data does not exist as an API, assemble it from what is public and auditable, and earn the rest through registry partnerships.

## Beat 3 — The workaround becomes the moat
The build spine comes together from OpenStreetMap Overpass, Overture Maps, national statistics offices, central banks, and the regional catastrophe-risk facility. Every value is tagged with its source and the moment it was fetched. What began as a workaround turns into the defensible asset: a provenance-tracked, multi-jurisdiction data spine that no one else has, plus a set of MoU letters to national registries that turn a hackathon project into an institutional roadmap.

## Beat 4 — Teaching the agents to be honest
The hardest engineering is not fetching data, it is trust. The team builds an agentic loop (research, verify, gate, sign-off) where every output carries an evidence class that caps its confidence, and where the system says "not enough data" instead of inventing an answer. A human sign-off gates every published verdict. The principle, written down and enforced in the math: honesty always wins over a confident guess.

## Beat 5 — From intelligence to advocacy
The same engine that values land can read a lease. The Lease & Contract Fairness Check flags clauses that break the law and cites the statute, giving an ordinary resident the kind of review that used to require a lawyer. The land-intelligence platform that funds the company and the advocacy tool that earns its trust are the same product seen from two sides.

## The through-line
Constraint drove design. No data API forced a provenance-first spine that became the moat. The requirement to be responsible forced calibrated honesty that became the product's signature. A solo builder, sponsor infrastructure end to end (built inside Shogo, running on Impala and MiniMax inference and Nebius compute), and a roadmap that a government could adopt on Monday.

## Pull-quotes (for social and press)
- "There was no land-data API to call, so we built the data spine from public records and tagged every cell with where it came from."
- "We taught the agents to say 'I do not have enough data' instead of guessing. Honesty is a feature."
- "The tool that values a parcel and the tool that protects a tenant are the same engine seen from two sides."
