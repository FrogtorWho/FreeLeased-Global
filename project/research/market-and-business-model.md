# FreeLeased — Market, PMF, Business Model, and GTM

## Market
- The Caribbean spans roughly thirteen sovereign jurisdictions plus dependencies, with a combined population near 44 million and a diaspora several times larger that buys, inherits, and disputes property from abroad.
- Property is a primary store of household wealth, yet title verification, valuation, and zoning interpretation are manual and slow, and land records are largely paper-based.
- The region is among the most disaster-exposed on earth. Climate and insurance risk is chronically mispriced, which raises the cost of capital for every development.
- Cross-border investment and financing are throttled by the absence of a shared, trustworthy property data layer.

## Who pays and why
- **Agencies and developers** need fast, defensible parcel, zoning, and valuation intelligence to price and close deals.
- **Lenders and insurers** need climate and title risk they can underwrite against.
- **Governments and registries** need to modernise records and keep sovereign data inside the country.
- **Residents** need to know a lease is lawful before they sign. This tier is free and drives trust and awareness.

## Business model
1. **Pro seats** (agencies, developers, lenders): per-seat monthly subscription for land intelligence, valuation support, climate and insurance risk.
2. **Data-spine API**: metered access for proptech and financial institutions.
3. **Self-hosted jurisdiction tier**: annual licence to governments, data stays in-country (sovereignty). High-value, sticky, and the on-ramp to registry feeds.
4. **Consumer fairness tier**: freemium. Top-of-funnel and mission; converts goodwill into policy access.

Revenue mix is designed so consumer advocacy earns trust and government licences earn data, while agencies and lenders pay the near-term bills.

## Product-market fit signals to capture (freeze week)
- One or two validation quotes from agencies, developers, or lenders via the Boardy benefit.
- Any response to the drafted registry MoUs.
- Diaspora demand: informal evidence of overseas owners struggling to verify or manage Caribbean property.

## Go-to-market
- **Beachhead:** a single jurisdiction with reachable open data and a motivated registry (Barbados or Trinidad and Tobago as primary candidates).
- **Wedge:** land intelligence for agencies and lenders (they pay quickly), with the free Fairness Check generating public awareness and political goodwill.
- **Expansion:** jurisdiction by jurisdiction via MoUs. Each new registry increases the value of the whole spine (data network effect).
- **Diaspora channel:** market the consumer tier to overseas owners who feel the pain most acutely and pay for certainty.

## Unit economics logic
The marginal cost of serving one more query is inference plus compute, both currently sponsor-subsidised and otherwise cheap on the open model. The expensive input is registry integration, which is amortised across every paying seat in that jurisdiction. Gross margin improves with each jurisdiction because the data spine is a shared fixed asset.

## Global relevance
Fragmented registries, mispriced climate risk, and opaque consumer contracts describe most small-island and emerging markets. A system hardened across thirteen Caribbean jurisdictions ports directly to the Pacific, West Africa, and Southeast Asia, which is the path from a regional company to a category-defining global one.
