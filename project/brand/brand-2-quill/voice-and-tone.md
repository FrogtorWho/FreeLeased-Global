# Voice & Tone — Quill

## Three things we always do

1. **Write like a feature article, not a product page.** Use long
   sentences. Use semicolons. Use the word "however" without
   apology. Our headlines can be 14 words if 14 is right.
2. **Cite like a footnote.** Every statute gets a footnote
   reference, superscripted, with a hover that opens the citation.
   The reader can scan the article or read the proof.
3. **Respect the reader's intelligence.** No "what is a lease?"
   explainers. Assume they know what an RTM notice looks like.

## Three things we never do

1. **Never use marketing superlatives.** "Best-in-class" is
   banned. "Leading" is banned. If the work is good, the work
   speaks; we don't shout over it.
2. **Never use jargon without unpacking it.** "s.20 consultation"
   must be glossed on first use — but only on first use. We trust
   the reader after that.
3. **Never talk down to landlords or tenants.** Quill is the
   newspaper of record, not the op-ed page.

## Tone by surface

| Surface | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing site | Feature article | "How a single lease clause can cost a leaseholder £18,000 — and what we built to stop it." |
| Product UI | Newspaper subhead | "Three clauses. Five sources. Two minutes." |
| Error states | Letter from the editor | "Page four failed to load. We'd like to try again." |
| Empty states | Quiet reassurance | "Nothing pending. The desk is clear." |
| HITL queue | Editorial dispatch | "Verdict filed. The desk has moved on." |

## What we sound like

The leader column of a quality weekend paper. The piece that runs
before the editorials. Confident, literate, never breathless.

## Vocabulary do's and don'ts

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| clause, provision, covenant | term, condition |
| resident, leaseholder, freeholder | tenant, landlord (vague) |
| filed, dispatched, decided | submitted, processed |
| attested, certified, sworn | confirmed, verified (we don't conflate) |
| first reading, second reading, engrossed | v1, v2, v3 |
